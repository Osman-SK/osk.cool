S.M.A.R.T. Monitoring Tools Part 2: Advanced Practical Metrics

July 18, 2026

Intro

There are a couple of very useful and practical metrics from SMART logs that you should be aware of. These metrics shine a light on how much a disk is used and consequently tells how much more usage is expected before the disk at question might fail.

We should also note that there is no perfect disk, all disks, whether designed for enterprise or home-use expected to fail at some point in the future. Regardless, the metrics we will discuss will help us determine the overall health in a more precise way than covered in the previous section [ 1 ].

The SMART Logs in Detail

When we run:

sudo smartctl -a /dev/sdb   # or any other disk

we are bombarded with lots of logs. If we do not know what to look for in general, it is easy to get lost.

Lets get an overall picture first, once we ran the command:

  1. INFORMATION SECTION
  2. READ SMART DATA SECTION

1- INFORMATION SECTION:

From the information section we get something like:


=== START OF INFORMATION SECTION ===

Model Family:     Seagate IronWolf
Device Model:     ST8000VN0022-2EL112
User Capacity:    8,001,563,222,016 bytes [8.00 TB]
SATA Version is:  SATA 3.1, 6.0 Gb/s (current: 6.0 Gb/s)

...

This section is about identification, capacity and capability of the drive in question, lets quickly go over them:

Family and Device model: is a quick check on whether we are looking at a disk we wanted; if you bought a second hand disk for example, this will tell whether the device is the same brand and model as advertised. Furthermore; from this information you can further do a search on these types of disks. Eg. their overall reliability; MTBF (mean hours to failure) etc.

Capacity is capacity,

SATA Version: Another useful info is whether the device is at a right SATA port on motherboard. Especially old PCs might have ports less than 6.0 Gb/s, say 3.0,

And if your device is capable of handling 6.0 Gb/s and depending on your use case, you might be losing speed because it is connected at a wrong port. This information is a good place to check.

2- READ SMART DATA SECTION:

Under this section, there is a table called: Vendor Specific SMART Attributes with Thresholds.

This is the crux of health assessment.

At first glance there are lots of columns (example table shows less for legibility), we shall focus on the most important columns:

  • TYPE
  • RAW_VALUE

Pre-fail Old_age etc.

RAW_VALUE gives the specific data for the metric.

And lastly the metrics:

  • #9 Power_On_Hours 
  • #241 Total_LBAs_Written

 

Please note that these metrics are not the whole story but still gives a quick idea.

#9 Power On Hours

This one simply shows how many hours the drive was up. Judge by yourself per table below:

Drive Type Typical "Comfortable" Range Notes / Real-World Data

Typical Desktop / Consumer (Barracuda, WD Blue, etc.)
Up to 15,000–25,000 hours (~2–3 years)
Not designed for 24/7. One 2026 study of failed drives showed median ~8,700 hours. In desktop (non-24/7) use they often last longer.

NAS disks (IronWolf, WD Red Plus/Pro, Toshiba N300)
30,000–60,000+ hours (3.5–7+ years)
Rated for 24/7. Backblaze sees many running reliably at 4–7 year average ages with low failure rates.

High-quality Enterprise (Seagate Exos, WD Ultrastar/Gold, Toshiba MG series)
50,000–80,000+ hours (6–9+ years)
Built for datacenters (MTBF 2–2.5 million hours). Backblaze fleet has many models averaging 5–7+ years old with stable ~1.3% AFR. Newer high-capacity models often perform even better.

#241 Total_LBAs_Written

Tracks the total number of Logical Block Addresses (sectors) written which in turn will give us the TeraBytes written value,

typically counted in 512-byte sectors so the calc goes:

TB written ≈ (RAW_VALUE × 512) / 1,000,000,000,000

In the example:

199823481677 x 512 / 1,000,000,000,000 = 102.31 TBs Written to disk.

Other Attributes

  • 12 Power_Cycle_Count: How many times the drive was powered on/off.

  • 194 Temperature_Celsius: Keep HDDs under ~50 °C sustained.

  • For SSDs: Wear-leveling / Media Wearout indicators, spare block counts.

Other important / critical attributes to watch (RAW_VALUE ideally stays at 0 for below):

  • 5 Reallocated_Sector_Ct: Sectors remapped to spares. Growth = dying drive (especially HDDs).
  • 197 Current_Pending_Sector: Unstable sectors waiting to be reallocated. Data is at risk.
  • 198 Offline_Uncorrectable: Sectors that failed even offline scans.
  • 187 Reported_Uncorrect: Uncorrectable errors reported to the OS.
  • 188 Command_Timeout: Often points to power or cabling issues.
  • 199 UDMA_CRC_Error_Count: SATA communication errors (bad cable/port).

Quick Summary – What Should Concern You?

Metric Desktop NAS (Standard) NAS Pro / Enterprise
Power On Hours >25k hours >50–60k hours >70–80k hours
Annual Writes (HDD) N/A >180 TB/yr >550 TB/yr

Critical SMART attributes: #5, #197, #198, #187 — these are the real early warnings
